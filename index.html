01 — A CLEARER PICTURE

UNDERSTANDING
WHAT'S HIDDEN.

Moisture problems are not always visible on the surface. Thermal imaging helps reveal unusual temperature patterns that may be associated with moisture intrusion, leakage pathways or insulation issues.

A thermal camera measures surface temperature — it does not see water directly. Our inspection process combines thermal observations with professional interpretation and additional verification wherever required.

09 — QUESTIONS, ANSWERED

GOOD TO
KNOW.

Understand what thermal scanning can — and cannot — tell you before booking.

Still have a question? Talk to us ↗
What is thermal scanning?+

Thermal scanning uses an infrared camera to measure surface temperatures and display temperature differences as an image. It can help identify patterns that need further investigation.

Can thermal scanning detect hidden water leakage?+

It can help identify unusual temperature patterns that may be associated with moisture. It does not detect water directly, and additional checks may be needed to confirm the cause.

Does thermal scanning damage walls?+

Thermal imaging itself is a non-contact inspection method and does not require drilling or breaking the scanned surface.

Can it help investigate bathroom leakage?+

Yes. Thermal observations around bathroom walls, floors and adjacent spaces may help identify areas for closer moisture investigation.

Can it help investigate roof leakage?+

It may help assess temperature patterns across roof and ceiling surfaces. Results depend on site conditions, timing and the nature of the suspected issue.

How long does an inspection take?+

Time depends on property size, access and the number of areas to inspect. Contact us with your property details for an estimate.

Do you provide inspection reports?+

Inspection findings can include observations and relevant thermal images. Please confirm the report format and scope when booking.

Does thermal scanning work in every weather condition?+

No. Ambient temperature, sunlight, wind, rain, surface materials and other conditions can affect readings. The inspector considers these factors when interpreting results.
